Rules behind the verdicts

  • A 30/60/90 plan works as a conversation tool: bring it out when the hiring manager asks the first question it answers, usually about priorities or the first months.
  • A generic plan hurts more than no plan. If it does not reflect this manager's priorities, hold it.
  • One page, three columns (30, 60, 90), four rows: focus, priorities, tasks, success measures.
  • Recruiter screens are for fit and logistics. Mention that the material exists; never present it there.
  • Panels score competencies question by question. Answer first; use one-page case write-ups as leave-behinds.
  • Show a digital tool for 60 seconds as method proof, never as the main story, and only with no personal data on screen.

5. Rehearse out loud
